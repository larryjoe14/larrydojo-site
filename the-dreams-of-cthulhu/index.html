the story

Before the oceans had names, something fell into them.

Lovecraft told it as a file of documents — a dead professor's notes, a sailor's log, a bas-relief nobody should have carved. The series tells it as the thing the documents circle: the dreams themselves.

Episode 1 goes back before the story's 1920s frame, to the account buried in its third chapter: the Great Old Ones came down from the stars when the Earth was young, raised a stone city in the sea, and when the geometry of the sky changed, sank with it — not dead, but dreaming.

"The Call of Cthulhu" entered the public domain along with the rest of Weird Tales' 1928 run — the same public-domain footing as Dick Tracy Origins. The adaptation owes no license; it owes the text.

The visual register is the book, not the blockbuster: dense crosshatching, a muted drowned-green palette, stone that reads as too large and too old, and light that comes only from what glows — runes, deep water, falling fire.

The captions carry Lovecraft's cadence in aged parchment boxes, set in old-style type. The panels move; the page stays a page.

the character

Built once. The same in every panel.

The dreamer himself was designed as a full turnaround before a single scene was drawn — front, three-quarter, and back — then composited into environment plates. That anchor discipline is what keeps a hand-inked style consistent shot to shot, where single-prompt AI drifts.

the style

Five rules, inked into every frame.

Dense Crosshatching

Shadow is drawn, not rendered. Every dark carries pen texture, so the frames read as printed plates.

Muted Color Palette

Drowned greens, bone, stone. Saturation is rationed so the glow means something when it comes.

Cyclopean Architecture

Stone at the wrong scale, angles slightly wrong on purpose. The city is a character, and it is too old.

Selective Bioluminescence

Light comes only from what should glow — runes, rifts, deep water. The only saturated color on screen.

Aged Parchment Captions

Narration lives in parchment boxes set in old-style calligraphy — the document register Lovecraft wrote in.
